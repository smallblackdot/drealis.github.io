Quick answers

Short takes on questions we hear a lot. Ask us for more detail anytime.

When should I start thinking about college?

Many students start exploring seriously in sophomore or junior year. Even earlier, you can focus on strong courses, activities you care about, and saving work that might go on a resume or activity list—no need to have everything figured out yet.

Do I need perfect grades to get in anywhere “good”?

No. Colleges look at your whole application. Strong essays, recommendations, and context (like course rigor at your school) matter. “Good fit” schools exist at many selectivity levels.

What is the FAFSA?

The Free Application for Federal Student Aid is how U.S. students apply for federal grants, work-study, and loans. Many schools also use it for their own aid. Always use official government or college sites for the real form and deadlines.

Is test-optional the same as test-blind?

No. Test-optional means you choose whether to send scores. Test-blind means the school does not consider SAT/ACT even if you send them. Check each college’s current policy on its admissions site.
